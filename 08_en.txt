8. Roll Call Area

The roll call area was the focal point of the prisoners’ camp. Seen from the gate, the barracks in which the prisoners were housed stood on the left. On the right were the laundry, kitchen, camp prison and infirmary.
Roll call took place two to three times a day on the roll call area. This was a way for the SS to check the number of prisoners. Each and every time, newly-arrived prisoners were recorded and the dead removed from the lists.
For the SS, the roll call was also a way to display the power they held over the prisoners. The inmates had to stand to attention in perfect rows, in all weathers, regardless of rain, snow or sun. In addition, members of the SS and the kapos regularly mistreated the prisoners. If, after the count, the target and actual numbers did not agree, the process could often go on for hours, or even through the night.
Stefan Krukowski from Poland remembers:
‘Another pastime were the roll calls that lasted for hours. Sometimes they were necessary, for example if there had been an escape. Then we stood there until the number of prisoners tallied [again] […]. Sometimes they were like a military drill. Mainly we were drilled when it was raining. The hours of standing around only came to an end when we were soaked through to the bone, when we could barely lift our feet from the ground, and when several candidates for the crematorium had been collected behind the barracks.’
