12. Fence and Ash Dump
The barbed-wire fence to the north of the camp is a reconstruction dating from 1947, which was created to given an impression of the position and dimensions of the original fence. The SS forced numerous prisoners to run into the fence. In the death registers, these murders were usually covered up using code words such as ‘suicide’ or ‘escape attempt’.
The survivor Ernst Martin reports on these staged murders:
‘The camp was surrounded by an electrified wire fence (380 volts). The prisoners were forced, usually new arrivals […], to run into this fence. The result was usually that the prisoner did not die straight away but got caught in the wire, burning, and suffered in agony for hours until he […] was either burnt to a cinder or else a shot then put him out of his misery. Here too you can say that there was no such thing as suicide but that, in reality, all who went into the wire were forced, with the exception of a very few who knew that they had to die and, in their despair, saw in this means of death the only way to shorten their suffering.’
Beyond the fence a memorial stone and a cross mark what is known as the ash dump. This was the site of a rubbish tip that, from 1944, was also used for the ashes of prisoners’ corpses burned in the crematorium ovens.
