21. Infirmary
Known as the ‘Krankenrevier’, the infirmary, this was the place where selected prisoners received medical treatment. To begin with it was housed in simple wooden barracks. At the end of 1944 this brick building, the so-called ‘new infirmary’, was finished and parts of it came into use. It was like a small hospital. With this expansion of medical treatment, the SS wanted to maintain its important prisoners’ ability to work – for example prisoner functionaries or skilled workers. The majority of inmates received little or no medical aid.
Yet injury and disease were part of everyday life in the camp. Meagre rations and hard physical labour resulted in emaciation. A lack of hygiene also promoted the spread of disease. Everyday violence and a lack of safety precautions at work repeatedly resulted in injury.
But the seriously ill and prisoners unable to work – if they were not a member of a privileged prisoner group – were considered useless. They were abused by SS doctors for medical experiments, murdered by lethal injection or in the gas chamber, or left to fend for themselves in separate parts of the camp.
The former infirmary has served as a museum building since 1970, having undergone major structural alterations.
On the ground floor you can visit the permanent exhibition ‘The History of the Mauthausen Concentration Camp 1938 – 1945’, and in the basement the exhibition ‘The Crime Scenes of Mauthausen – Searching for Traces’. The next station on this audio guide can be found in the basement at the end of the exhibition.
