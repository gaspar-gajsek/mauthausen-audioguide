9. Barracks No. 1
The SS gave a small number of concentration camp prisoners power over their fellow inmates. Known as prisoner functionaries or kapos, they were responsible for maintaining order in the camp and many were brutal and violent in doing so. In return, the SS granted these prisoners better rations and accommodation as well as other privileges.
This system allowed the SS to keep administration and guard numbers down. At the same time, the unequal treatment of prisoners undermined solidarity between them.
The right-hand side of barracks 1 housed the camp clerks’ office. A separate work detachment of clerks had to keep exact records of prisoners’ personal data and status. The camp clerks assigned the prisoners to work detachments and subcamps. This gave them considerable influence over the prison conditions of their fellow inmates.
In the centre of the barracks was the ‘canteen’. Here, as a ‘productivity incentive’, the prisoner functionaries and other privileged inmates could purchase goods such as tobacco, personal hygiene articles and, very occasionally, additional foodstuffs.
The left-hand side of the barracks was the prisoner brothel from 1942 onwards. Here prisoners from the Ravensbrück women’s concentration camp were sexually exploited. They were promised release from the concentration camp in return for working in the brothel – a promise that was never kept. A visit to the brothel was only granted to a very few privileged prisoners.
