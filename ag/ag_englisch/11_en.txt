11. Jewish Barrack
From 1941 to 1944, prisoners categorised as Jews were housed in Room B of barracks 5. They were isolated from all other prisoners and were at the very bottom of the camp hierarchy. They received fewer rations, poorer clothing and no form of protection from the cold in the winter. They were also denied any medical treatment. Jewish prisoners were assigned to particularly arduous work detachments. Under these conditions, most died just a few days or weeks after their arrival. Others were deliberately murdered. For example they were shot during staged ‘escape attempts’, were pushed over the edge of the quarry, were forced to run into the electric fence or – like many other prisoners no longer able to work – were murdered by poison gas at the Hartheim Castle killing facility.
Josef Herzler from Vienna, one of the few survivors of what was known as the ‘Jewish block’, remembers:
‘Within the camp, the block was specially fenced in again with barbed wire. Coming back from work we had to go straight to the block; there was no chance of coming into contact with the other prisoners. […] The sleeping arrangements […] were in so-called “sardine form”, which meant there were no plank[s] to lie on, no blankets, we had to spend the night head to toe, toe to head, squashed together on our sides on the bare floor. We lay on our threadbare prisoner clothing.’
