6. SS and Headquarters
Guarding and running the concentration camps was the responsibility of the Schutzstaffel – meaning ‘guard detachment’ – or ‘SS’ for short. It was the armed wing of the NSDAP, or National Socialist German Workers’ Party. After the National Socialists seized power, it was assigned policing and military tasks.
The area covered by today’s memorial park was originally an area used by the Mauthausen camp SS. Alongside several guard and office barracks, the headquarters were also located here. This was the main office of the former camp administration. It is the only building remaining today.
The commandant was the head of the camp SS. Below him were the SS officers, non-commissioned officers and guards. As the number of prisoners grew, so too did the number of SS personnel. By the end of the war, nearly 8,000 men were involved in guarding the main camp and subcamps.
For the high-ranking SS officers, a separate residential estate was built not far from the camp where they lived with their families. There were lots of different types of contact between the resident population and members of the SS such as economic relations, joint festivities and marriages. But also pub brawls and other forms of conflict.
For the seven years of its existence, only two commandants worked at the Mauthausen concentration camp: Albert Sauer held the post until early 1939. Franz Ziereis, his successor, remained commandant until liberation. He was shot and wounded whilst trying to flee the Allied forces and was brought back to the Gusen concentration camp. Before he died, he was interrogated and admitted to the mass murders committed at Mauthausen. However, he tried to shift responsibility onto superiors and subordinates alike.
