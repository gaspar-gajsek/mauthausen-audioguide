2. Construction of the Camp
The high wall in front of you separated the part where the inmates were held – the prisoners’ camp – from other areas.
Construction of the Mauthausen concentration camp began in August 1938 – just a few months after the annexation of Austria to National Socialist Germany. The first prisoners were transferred from Dachau concentration camp. At first the camp consisted of only wooden barracks and an electric fence. The imposing stone buildings and the enclosure wall were not built until later. Today, only a few parts of the former prisoners’ camp still exist. There also used to be barracks around where the visitor centre is now located. These were used by the SS as offices, workshops and living quarters.
Prisoners were usually transported to Mauthausen in large groups and by train – often in freight trains or cattle trucks. From the station they were forced to march the four kilometres through the town up to the concentration camp – under constant harassment and beatings from the guards. Many already had a long history of persecution and imprisonment behind them. They had been transferred either from other camps or from prison. Following long journeys, they often arrived in Mauthausen exhausted or ill.
