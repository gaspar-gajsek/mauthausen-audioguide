16. Quarantine Yard
This yard, an area separated from the rest of the camp by a stone wall, where barracks 16 to 20 were located, was always used to isolate particular groups of prisoners. To begin with the sick were housed here in the so-called ‘Sonderrevier’, the ‘special infirmary’, separated from the other concentration camp prisoners, then Soviet prisoners of war. For a long time it was the site of what was known as ‘quarantine’.
All newly arrived prisoners had to spend between two and four weeks in the quarantine camp. This was supposed to prevent the spread of infectious diseases. It was also the period during which the prisoners got used to the terror in the camp and learned the rules of behaviour they would need to survive. ‘Quarantine’ also provided an early opportunity to weed out the sick and the weak. With 1,000 or more prisoners being held in them, the barracks of the quarantine camp were usually completely overcrowded, with neither beds nor blankets. Mistreatment by block functionaries was the order of the day. The hostile prison conditions led to a large number of deaths among the prisoners crowded together here.
