4. Quarry
The granite quarries in Mauthausen and Gusen were the reason that the SS established concentration camps in these places. In order to run and exploit the quarries for economic gain, it had founded its own company: the ‘Deutsche Erd- und Steinwerke GmbH’ or German Earth and Stone Works Company. The firm profited from the back-breaking labour of the concentration camp prisoners and produced enormous profits. The Mauthausen quarry, together with the one in the Gusen branch camp four kilometres away, soon formed a giant industrial complex.
The quarry was also a site of extermination. The so-called stone carrying work detachment, also known as the ‘Strafkompanie’, the ‘penal company’, was forced to carry granite blocks weighing up to 50 kilos up the quarry steps to the camp. They were therefore called the ‘stairs of death’ by the inmates. Prisoners were also deliberately killed here. They were shot or pushed to their deaths over the edge of the quarry. For the latter, the SS used the cynical term ‘parachutist’.
The Polish survivor Stefan Niewiada witnessed this kind of event:
‘I saw crimes committed against a group of Dutch Jews. The group consisted of around 100 people. They were lined up one after the other and had to push each other off the cliff. I was working 150 metres away from the place where they came crashing down. Afterwards the penal company took the corpses to the crematorium.’
From 1943 onwards, production in the quarries gradually came to a halt as the arms industry took priority. Aeroplane parts were now produced in large workshops next to the quarries. These workshops no longer exist today.
