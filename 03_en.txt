3. Infirmary Camp
The field with trees you see today was, at the time of the camp, a site of mass death. This was the site of the so-called “Sanitätslager” – the infirmary camp. It was originally established in 1941 as a camp for Soviet prisoners of war. It consisted of several wooden barracks surrounded by an electric fence. By the time it was finished in summer 1943, the majority of the Soviet prisoners of war were already dead. Therefore the camp was used to accommodate sick prisoners who were no longer able to work. Many of them had been transferred back to Mauthausen concentration camp from one of the 40 or more subcamps. Thousands were housed here without sufficient food or medical supplies and left to die.
The survivor August Kammhuber from Vienna speaks about the infirmary camp:
‘Every day I saw dozens of people keel over dead without being in a position to offer them even the smallest amount of help. […] No day went by without dozens of the dead being thrown out from among the half-living, for the place of a dead man was to be filled again right away.’
Almost directly bordering the infirmary camp, on the left-hand side, was a place used by the SS for their own enjoyment: The football pitch here was used regularly for matches, often against teams from the region. Local civilians were also allowed to come to these matches, indirectly making them witnesses to the mass death in the infirmary camp.
