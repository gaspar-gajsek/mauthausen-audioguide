14. Kitchen Barrack
In the kitchen barracks, a prisoner work detachment prepared meals for the SS and for the concentration camp prisoners. Whilst rations for the guards were good, the mass of prisoners remained systematically under-fed. Even in the early years, when concentration camp rations were still better, the – often stale – food contained 1,500 calories at most – and this despite the fact that the prisoners were generally forced to carry out hard physical labour. In the months leading up to the liberation of the camp, the lack of food led to mass death on a dramatic scale.
The SS let the concentration camp prisoners go hungry on purpose in order to weaken their will to live and increase the struggle for survival between them. Only getting hold of additional food would ensure survival. Prisoner functionaries were given better rations. Some groups of prisoners were allowed to receive food packages from relatives. In order not to starve, the inmates pursued their own strategies.
The former prisoner Hans Maršálek writes:
‘The feeling of hunger hounded you day and night. The famished talked about food at every possible opportunity. […] They traded: traded two slices of sausage or bread for soup […], underwear or clothing for food. They scoured their workplaces for anything edible, ate roots, grass, leaf buds, acorns, rats, cats, dogs, all types of rubbish and brown coal. They attacked the food carriers or tried to lick drops of turnip stew from empty pans. […] At night they tried to steal bread.’
