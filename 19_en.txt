19. Camp II
This area surrounded by a stone wall, where the mortal remains of nearly 3,000 prisoners who died after liberation are now buried, was created in 1941. Initially there were workshops here and from 1944 onwards it was used as a quarantine area. During this period, the prisoners arriving on mass transports had to spend their first weeks here under harsh conditions.
This part of the camp, known as ‘Camp II’, became a centre of organised prisoner resistance in the months before liberation. Starting in 1944, a group of political prisoners were able to build up a secret organisation. At first their aim was to create a network for providing prisoners in need with rations and to hide particularly vulnerable inmates from the SS. In the weeks before liberation, the group also tried to safeguard evidence and prepare for the possibility of armed combat with the SS.
However, resistance in a concentration camp cannot be reduced to this form of organised defence by a few. Solidarity with fellow inmates, maintaining the will to survive and holding on to self respect were also a part of it. In a place where dehumanisation is the aim and the norm, any attempt to preserve humanity must be seen as an act of resistance.
