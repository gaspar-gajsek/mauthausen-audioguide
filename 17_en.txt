17. Women's Camp
From the beginning of 1945, barracks 16 to 18 were no longer used as a quarantine camp. Following the arrival of just under 2,000 women and children from the Ravensbrück women’s concentration camp, the SS designated these barracks a so-called women’s camp.
With very few exceptions, until then only men had been imprisoned in the main camp at Mauthausen. The first 3,000 or so female prisoners had already been officially registered in what was termed the Mauthausen women’s concentration camp as early as September 1944. However, they worked in subcamps specially set up for the war industry.
In the final weeks before liberation, further large groups of women arrived at Mauthausen from evacuated concentration camps. Some were deported on to other camps, others were housed in barracks in the quarries and infirmary camp.
In total, some 10,000 women are thought to have been imprisoned in the Mauthausen camp system.
