7. Arrival
The camp gatehouse was the only entrance to the prisoners’ camp. Consequently it was strictly guarded and controlled. Newly-arrived inmates entered the prisoners’ camp for the first time through this gate. This was followed by a lengthy admission procedure that was always accompanied by torture and harassment. For the prisoner it represented an utter break with their life experience up until then.
The prisoners were forced to line up along the camp wall. They were often left standing there for several hours, regardless of the burning summer sun or winter frosts. Many died of exhaustion or from beatings by the kapos or SS. In camp jargon, this section of the wall was given the name ‘Wailing Wall’.
After they had been registered and their clothing and belongings had been taken from them, the prisoners were led into the basement of the laundry barracks opposite.
Alberto Todros, an Italian, recalls what happened here:
‘They take us to some stairs that lead into the basement of the barracks. Clothing, suitcases, parcels remain lying in a heap outside. Those of us at the front go down the stairs, come to a […] room in which other prisoners shave all the hair from our bodies, without any soap and using completely blunt shaving tools, quick, quick, one after another. In this state they push us into a second room where the showers are. As soon as the room is full, water comes out and they order us to wash ourselves, then we leave by a second door and come to a pile of shirts and underwear. […] The skinny are given clothing as loose as possible, the short the longest possible. We look at each other and barely recognise ourselves any more … We have no words for what has happened to us.’
