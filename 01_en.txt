1. Mauthausen Camp System
The site map, which you can find on your information guide, gives you an overview of the Mauthausen Memorial complex. By entering the number shown into your audio guide, you can listen to information about individual sites and about topics from the history of the Mauthausen concentration camp.
Between 1938 and 1945, the Mauthausen concentration camp was part of a network of concentration camps that stretched across the entire German Reich and, later on, also across parts of the occupied territories. At first these camps were used to imprison political opponents and members of other social groups considered undesirable by the National Socialists within the German Reich. However, after the start of the war, the majority of prisoners came from the occupied territories. They were deported to the concentration camps not only on political, but also on racist grounds.
Most of the prisoners in the Mauthausen concentration camp were from Poland and the Soviet Union. Many also came from countries in southern and western Europe. In total, 40 different nations were represented among the prisoners.
In the early years, the inmates were interned in the main camp at Mauthausen and in the Gusen branch camp. The majority worked as forced labourers in the quarries. Starting in 1942, a network of over 40 subcamps emerged. Most of the prisoners in the subcamps were used as slave labour for the arms industry.
